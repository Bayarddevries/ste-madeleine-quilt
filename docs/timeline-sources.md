## Book quotes — line-by-line provenance

### Quote 1 (Stage 1 — homeland bond)
> "...it had something to do with the land. Although it was never articulated, the implied bond was homeland."

- Source file line range: 194–198
- Speaker: Zeilig & Zeilig, narrator (preface/chapter 1)

### Quote 2 (1885→1913 gap — settlement roots)
> "...when the Metis of the Red River Valley were forced to abandon their settlement after 1870, it was logical they would choose a familiar area in which to make their new home. Also, many Metis who had settled in Saskatchewan, chose to return to Manitoba, following the Rebellion of 1885."

- Source file line range: 284–288
- Speaker: Zeilig & Zeilig, narrator (chapter 1, settlement origins)

### Quote 3 (1885→1913 gap — 1913 chapel petition, PRIMARY SOURCE)
> "We the undersigned residents of the Mission of Ste. Madeleine... now that we have constructed, by our own means, a small chapel... send us a missionary who is able to speak our Indian languages."

- Source file line range: 5910–5935
- Original document date: February 2, 1913
- Speaker: Residents of Ste. Madeleine Mission (petition letter to Archbishop Langevin)
- Provenance: letter reproduced in *Ste. Madeleine* pp. 184–185, written in the hand of Alfred Lemay
- Attribution on board: `— Ste. Madeleine residents, Feb 2 1913, in Zeilig & Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town, 1987`

### Quote 4 (Stage 2 — displacement / homes burned)
> "...their homes were burned, their dogs were shot in full view of everyone, and they were turned off the land without compensation."

- Source file line range: 173–175
- Speaker: Zeilig & Zeilig narrator paraphrasing MMF President Yvon Dumont
- Note: Dumont's story as told to Z&Z in spring 1986. Use Z&Z as the citation; Dumont is the named original source quoted within.

### Quote 5 (Stage 3 — cemetery / mound of grass)
> "...all that remains of Ste. Madeleine are the stone foundations of the Belliveau School, and the cemetery encircling the mound of grass where the church once stood."

- Source file line range: 372–374
- Speaker: Zeilig & Zeilig, narrator (chapter on displacement aftermath)

**Quote 6 (Stage 4 — Chartrand land return):**
> "This might be the first time... that the land — a piece of land — is given back to us that was robbed from us."

- Attribution on board: `— David Chartrand, MMF President, July 19 2024`

### Will Goodon — July 2023 Ste. Madeleine Métis Days

**Full citation:**
> Red River Métis bring Ste. Madeleine to life for Métis Days. Manitoba Métis Federation Spotlight, July 18, 2023. https://www.mmf.mb.ca/mmf-spotlight/red-river-metis-bring-ste-madeleine-to-life-for-metis-days

**Quote 7 (Stage 5a — culture survives):**
> "...The culture that was supposedly burned when the houses were burnt, but it never was. We kept it inside ourselves... It's that culture that is keeping us alive, keeps us coming back."

- Speaker: Minister Will Goodon, MMF Southwest Region Executive
- Attribution on board: `— Will Goodon, MMF Southwest Region, July 2023`

### Gail Welburn — July 2024 MMF Spotlight

**Full citation:**
> From ashes to honour: Red River Métis reclaim Ste. Madeleine. Manitoba Métis Federation Spotlight, July 25, 2024. https://www.mmf.mb.ca/mmf-spotlight/from-ashes-to-honour-red-river-metis-reclaim-ste-madeleine

**Quote 8 (Stage 5b — closing / come full circle):**
> "It's come full circle. It's come back today. I'm here today in respect and recognition of what my grandpa went through on this land."

- Speaker: Gail Welburn, descendant of Ste. Madeleine (grandfather displaced at age 27)
- Attribution on board: `— Gail Welburn, descendant, July 2024`
