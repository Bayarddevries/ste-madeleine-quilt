Métis families, pushed out of Red River by the Wolseley expedition, settle at Ste. Madeleine

 

1870

More families arrive after the North-West Resistance, seeking land that won't be taken again

1885

The one-room Belliveau School was built for Grades One through Eight. Lessons were sometimes taught in French and English.

1922

 

The municipality orders the families out; homes are burned, dogs shot. Joe Venne and others confront the crew with rifles and save the church. The Vermette family moves in to guard it

1938

 

Local Métis families built the log chapel by hand so that church services could be held once or twice a month in Ste. Madeleine.

1913

The federal Prairie Farm Rehabilitation Act passes; Ste. Madeleine is designated as community pasture

1935

 

A census counts 170 Métis still in the Binscarth area

1958

An MOU returns 100 acres to the Manitoba Métis Federation; a new cross is raised in the cemetery

2024

 

The community gathers on the land for Ste. Madeleine Days 2026 to celebrate the reclamaition of the historic space

2026

 

1958-2024

…it had something to do with the land. Although it was never articulated, the implied bond was homeland.

— K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 7

…their homes were burned, their dogs were shot in full view of everyone, and they were turned off the land without compensation.

— Y. Dumont, recounted in K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 4

…all that remains of Ste. Madeleine are the stone foundations of the Belliveau School, and the cemetery encircling the mound of grass where the church once stood.

— K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 4

GENERATIONS OF WAITING

This might be the first time… that the land — a piece of land — is given back to us that was robbed from us.

— David Chartrand, MMF President, July 19 2024

…The culture that was supposedly burned when the houses were burnt, but it never was. We kept it inside ourselves… It’s that culture that is keeping us alive, keeps us coming back.

— Will Goodon, MMF Southwest Region, July 2023

“It’s come full circle. It’s come back today. I’m here today in respect and recognition of what my grandpa went through on this land.”

— Gail Welburn, descendant, July 2024

Community members gathered behind Beliveau School, which no longer exists, ca early 1900s; Courtesy of the Manitoba Museum.

...when the Metis of the Red River Valley were forced to abandon their settlement after 1870, it was logical they would choose a familiar area in which to make their new home. Also, many Metis who had settled in Saskatchewan, chose to return to Manitoba, following the Rebellion of 1885.

— K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 3

"We the undersigned residents of the Mission of Ste. Madeleine... now that we have constructed, by our own means, a small chapel... send us a missionary who is able to speak our Indian languages."

— Ste. Madeleine residents, Feb 2 1913, in K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 184

"They built log houses on the homesteads—and log barns—and moved in there."

— Joe Venne (b. 1906), in K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 22

...They would have been home. When they die, they still come and get buried there.

— Lazare Fouillard, in K. & V. Zeilig, Ste. Madeleine: Community Without a Town — Métis Elders in Interview, 1987, p. 190
